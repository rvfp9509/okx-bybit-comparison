## Derivatives: where the Bybit comparison gets interesting

This is the category where "best Bybit alternative" is hardest to call. Bybit built its reputation on derivatives, and the order book depth on major perps — BTC, ETH, SOL — is genuinely strong. OKX competes here with a different structure.

OKX supports three margin types for perpetuals: USDT-margined, USDC-margined, and coin-margined. It also offers expiry futures with quarterly and bi-quarterly settlement, which Bybit doesn't emphasize. Maximum leverage on the most liquid perpetual contracts goes up to 125x, though the actual ceiling depends on position size and the specific contract. For comparison, Bybit also offers high leverage on perps, with limits that vary by product.

Where OKX has a structural edge is the Liquid Marketplace and RFQ (Request for Quote) system, which lets you execute multi-leg strategies and block trades with a dedicated OTC desk. For traders running complex structures — calendar spreads, combinations of perps and options, large block trades that would slip badly on a public order book — this is a real differentiator. Bybit has its own OTC rails, but OKX's RFQ builder is more developed for multi-leg work.

Options are a similar story. Both exchanges list European-style options on BTC and ETH. OKX integrates options into the same RFQ system, which matters if you're doing combos rather than single-leg directional bets. For most users, options are a secondary product, but if you trade them seriously, OKX's setup is worth a look.

One honest caveat: Bybit still feels more trader-first in the perps UI. The chart layout, the order entry flow, the way positions display — it's built for someone who lives in the derivatives tab. OKX's interface is cleaner but more modular, which is better if you jump between spot, futures, Earn, and the wallet, and slightly less focused if you don't.

## Security and proof of reserves

Neither exchange should be called "safe" in an absolute sense. Both are custodial platforms, which means custody risk is real regardless of how good the security stack is.

OKX publishes monthly Proof of Reserves using zk-STARK zero-knowledge proofs, which lets users independently verify on-chain that their balances are backed 1:1. The exchange reports that over 95% of user assets are held in multi-signature offline cold wallets. OKX has not suffered a major exchange-level hack, though it did plead guilty in February 2025 to operating an unlicensed money transmitting business in the U.S. and agreed to pay over $504 million in penalties — a compliance event that affects product availability in some regions.

Bybit also publishes Proof of Reserves and maintained 1:1 backing through the February 2025 hack, but the hack itself — $1.5 billion stolen by Lazarus — is the kind of operational security failure that resets risk perception. Bybit covered the loss and continued operating, but the event is now part of the platform's history.

For long-term holdings, neither is the right answer. Keep only trading balances on exchange and move the rest to a hardware wallet. This isn't a hedge — it's the standard practice for anyone holding meaningful crypto value.
